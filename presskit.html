One line

A modern spiritual successor to SkyRoads: five lanes of road suspended in space, 25 levels that never explain themselves, and a level editor you have to beat your own level to publish in.

One paragraph

GRAVLANE is a fast, brutal arcade racer set on a road suspended in open space. Four worlds carry you from a dying sun, through the wreckage of dead stars, into a place where gravity stops obeying the rules and out the far side of reality. Nineteen tile types — fire, crumbling ground, zero-gravity fields, magnets, ghost tiles that are only pictures of tiles — and the game explains none of them in advance. You learn what a tile does by surviving it. Death restarts you in about a second, so failure costs almost nothing and the levels are built to be memorised rather than reacted to. Every soundtrack is synthesized live while you play, which is why the blinking tiles pulse on the music's actual beat. It is free, carries no advertising and sells nothing.

Features

  • 25 levels across four worlds, plus four hidden gauntlet levels that are meaner than anything in the story
  • 19 tile types the game refuses to explain — each one is logged in TILE DATA the first time you survive it
  • Live-synthesized soundtracks. Every level's music is generated as you play, and the blinking tiles read the same clock as the synth
  • Eight ships and a garage: paint, finishes, hull, wing and exhaust geometry, and stat upgrades bought with earned cores. Four of the ships are hidden and nothing tells you where
  • THE FORGE — a full level editor that opens once you clear the story. Paint tiles, heights, obstacles and tunnels, choose a backdrop and a soundtrack. You must beat your own level before you are allowed to publish it
  • Community levels, with in-app reporting and creator blocking
  • Google Play leaderboards and achievements, with a best time recorded per level
  • Touch, tilt or gamepad
  • No assets. Every graphic, ship, star and note is generated in code at runtime — nothing is downloaded

About the developer

GRAVLANE is the work of one person. It was built because SkyRoads (Bluemoon Interactive, 1993) never got the successor it deserved, and it is free because it was never meant to be a business.
